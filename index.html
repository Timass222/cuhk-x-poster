Timestamps are a free sensor

What the session decoder changes, step by step. Real hidden-test clips; about 1 minute, no sound.

We recognise 40 daily activities from depth, infrared, skeleton and IMU clips, without RGB, on people never seen in training.

Clip start times chain the clips into sessions. A second-order decoder labels the whole session with no test labels: 75.8 → 86.4 % on 405 hidden-test clips.

About 3/4 of that gain comes from the order of everyday actions. The whole model is 94 MiB.

How the decoder result is computed

For every clip, each of the 40 classes gets

score(c) ∝ clip(c)0.85 × T(a, b → c)

T(a, b → c) ∝ (0.75·P2(c | a, b) + 0.25·P1(c | b))0.75

P1 and P2 are counted on the training lists, given the last one or two actions. The power 0.75 and renormalising over the 40 classes flatten them, so unseen transitions keep a small chance. Forward–backward runs this over the whole session, averaging over uncertain earlier clips and looking at the next clip too.

Example, step 1 of the video (one action back only, so T ∝ P10.75). After Peel fruits, Eat food has P1 = 0.545 → 0.5450.75 = 0.634 → 0.34 after renormalising. The clip alone gives Eat food 0.1160.85 = 0.16 and Use a mobile phone 0.3820.85 = 0.44. Products: 0.16 × 0.34 = 0.054 and 0.44 × 0.011 = 0.005, which over all 40 classes is 0.74 vs 0.07. Full forward–backward gives 0.72 vs 0.07.
